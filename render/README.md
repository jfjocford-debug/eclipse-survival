# Canvas rendering

`new Renderer(canvas, world)` implements `resize()`, `render(state, alpha)`, `worldToScreen(x,y,z=0)`, `screenToWorld(cssX,cssY)`, `setZoom(value)`, and `destroy()`. The default zoom is 1.18 and the supported range is 0.55–2.1. Projection coordinates are always CSS pixels, independent of device pixel ratio. The renderer measures the canvas's CSS bounds and caps its backing-store density at 2×.

The camera follows the player. Projection uses 64×32 ground diamonds and 32 pixels per metre of height. Integer prop x/y values are tile origins. Rotation 0 follows the x axis; rotation 1 or 90 follows y. East and south `cutaway` walls are low walls. Tile `floorStyle`, `color`, and `rug` plus prop `variant`, `color`, `open`, `barricaded`, `looted`, `lit`, `w`, `h`, and `rotation` affect the artwork. Tall trees fade when they cover the survivor.

- `primitives.js`: deterministic visual hashes, colors, polygons, and shaded isometric geometry.
- `tiles.js`: cached material textures, including worn floorboards, tile/checker floors, concrete, grass, dirt, and cracked asphalt.
- `props.js`: cached, detailed furniture and street props, with refrigerators, sinks, stocked/looted containers, functioning doors and boards, pine/oak trees, and lights.
- `characters.js`: eight supplied survivor facings, 32 cached desaturated/bloodied infected variants derived from the same anatomy, plus original directional fallback figures, equipped weapons, and bodies.
- `renderer.js`: depth sorting, shadows, room rugs and litter, road markings, camera/zoom, light masks, window shafts, warm lamp glows, rain/fog, damage effects, and interaction outlines.

Particle physics remain owned by the combat system. The renderer reads `player.attackTimer/attackDuration/hurtTimer`, zombie `hitTimer/health/deathTimer`, `state.lastAttack`, and particle `x/y/z/life/maxLife/type/size/color`. It does not mutate simulation state. Blood decals and walk phases are transient render caches; persistent actor/world data remains owned by the game.

All texture generation happens in local canvases and all source art ships with the game. Static terrain, road paint, rugs and litter are baked into one opaque viewport-sized ground canvas with a 192px margin on each side. The cache rebuilds beyond 128px of camera movement or on zoom, resize, or world replacement. `renderer.cacheGround = false` enables the equivalent direct path for profiling. Props and actors retain independent depth sorting. Each prop sprite is alpha-cropped once when baked, with one pixel of antialias padding and adjusted projection anchors; environments without pixel reads retain the uncropped sprite. The prop cache is bounded to 420 entries; destroyed renderer caches are released. `renderer.stats` exposes visible source counts and atlas sizes for profiling. The render pass respects reduced-motion settings.

Verification: `node --test render/renderer.test.mjs` checks inverse projection with multiple zooms/cameras and DPR resizing, renders real generated neighborhoods through a validating Canvas API mock across every prop type and combat/weather branch, checks PNG asset signatures and dimensions, verifies the bounded infected variant cache, checks ground cache reuse/invalidation on camera pan, zoom, resize and world replacement, and verifies alpha-crop padding/anchor math and restricted-canvas fallbacks. This is geometry/runtime validation, not a browser visual assertion. The integration QA agent owns browser checks.

Prototype limits: lighting uses composited radial masks and ambient/contact shadows rather than physical ray tracing. Rain/fog and the day/night cycle are presentation effects driven by simulation state. The survivor's supplied art has eight directional idle views with a walking sway; infected views use a hunched transform and uneven sway, with a four-phase limb animation fallback. No copyrighted commercial game assets or external runtime libraries are included.
