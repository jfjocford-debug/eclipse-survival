# Design pillars (carried over from the Eclipse Survival archive, decided Oct 10, 2026)

Setting: the early 1990s. The threat is called **the RNVs**, short for **Red Night Visitors** (decided Oct 12, 2026), a name that reads as an early-90s acronym. The old working name from the archive is retired.

North-star test for every feature: **does it create a decision or a consequence worth remembering? If it only creates labour, cut or automate it.**

1. **Slow dread.** Tension comes from attrition, noise, light and the rhythm of the clock, not from constant fighting.
2. **Earned, explained death.** Permadeath stays harsh, but death follows a decision and the player can read why. No death from a single unlucky roll. A wound starts a visible fuse (bleeding, then infection) with a timer and a way to treat it; a death recap names the causes.
3. **Chores become decisions.** Waiting, hauling and repetition collapse into single actions. Choices about risk, supplies and people stay.
4. **A small, alive simulation.** A few interlocking systems chosen for the stories they create, not a long feature list.
5. **A place that feels real.** A believable town, believable interiors, loot that makes sense where it is found.
6. **The twist, and it is rare.** One overwhelming threat (the Apostles in the archive) that you avoid, outlast or outsmart until you are a mastered late-game player, and even then it is not an easy fight. The Eclipse that brings it must be **rare**: so far apart that the player can forget it exists, until it arrives and the player truly might not survive the night. Rarity is the point; it must never become a routine event. The RNVs need an original look as well as an original name before the game is published: no borrowed silhouette.

Design tension to resolve (pillar 2 against pillar 6): a night that may kill you must still be an *earned* death. The answer is a short, readable omen before it begins and real tools to prepare, hide or outlast it, so that survival or failure traces back to the player's preparation, never to a surprise roll.

## Where Deadfall stands against them (from the handoff; confirm in play)

| Pillar | Already in Deadfall | Missing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day and night, weather, noise from running and gunfire, hunger, thirst, energy | Vision limits (see VISIBILITY.md) |
| 2 | Bleeding and infection as ongoing conditions | A visible fuse timer, named damage sources, death recap |
| 3 | Rest action | A single safe "pass time" action that stops when something happens |
| 4 | Needs, crafting, barricades, skills | Nothing to cut yet; decide before adding |
| 5 | Nine named buildings, furnished interiors, streamed town | Interior rooms; lived-in detail |
| 6 | Ordinary zombies only | The rare Eclipse night, the threat, and its omen |

Planned order: vision (VISIBILITY.md) first, the fuse and death recap second, the twist and its clock third.

## Omens (direction from Jacques, Oct 12; examples, not final)

Subtle signs that an Eclipse night is approaching, beyond a short readable warning. Directions to explore: normally hostile enemies standing in strange, cult-like formations and chanting; wildlife falling from the sky at the player's feet; resting that ends with the player waking, screen black, somewhere nearby that is not where they lay down, belongings in a neat pile beside them.

Rules to keep so omens serve pillar 2 instead of breaking it:
- Omens never lie. An omen always means the Eclipse is coming, so the player can learn them across runs.
- Omens escalate over several in-game days, from faint to unmistakable, and the last stage leaves time to act.
- Uncanny is not harmful. The wake-up-elsewhere event costs the player nothing: no loss of items, no placement into danger.
- Omens are cheap to produce: seeded, static scenes and one-off events, not new simulation.
